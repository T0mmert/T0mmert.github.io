Two machines

Two old HP Z240 small-form-factor desktops, rebuilt as servers at home.

apollo has the Core i5-6600, 32 GB of memory and two Tesla P4 graphics cards. It runs Ubuntu Server, Ollama with the Qwen3 4B and 8B models split across both cards, and the agent layer for Apollo, the assistant it's named after.

media has 16 GB and a 2 TB drive. It runs OpenMediaVault and Jellyfin, a film library for the house. Next, its services move into Docker Compose, so the whole machine can be rebuilt from one file in Git, and then it hosts my own websites.

Cooling cards that have no fan

The Tesla P4 is a datacentre card: it has no fan of its own and counts on the airflow of a server case. A small desktop has none, so I designed a bracket that holds a 40 × 20 mm fan against the cards, and 3D-printed it. Both cards are low-profile and draw at most 75 watts each, which is what lets two of them fit this case and its power supply.

Old cards have an end date

The newest driver series that supports these cards is also the last one. That limits how long they stay useful, and I hadn't weighed it when I chose them. The driver is now held at that series, both cards are checked after every kernel update, and remaining driver support is part of choosing hardware from here on.

The rules

  • Nothing faces the internet until it has to. Everything runs on the home network, and exposing a service is the last step, not the first.
  • Every change starts with a backup, and a backup only counts once restoring it has been tested.
  • The assistant's workspace, models and configuration are never reinstalled casually: they're the part that takes longest to get right.

Left out on purpose

Addresses, ports and software versions aren't on this page. They're the first thing someone would look for, and you don't need them to see how it's put together.
