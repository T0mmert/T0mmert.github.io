A four-page marketing site, in Dutch, for a staff-scheduling product whose whole pitch is honesty. So the site shows everything: the price, the rules and the product itself.

The brief

Shiftbay plans rosters and records hours for teams whose shifts change every week: restaurants and shops, care, cleaning and security. Its pitch is transparency. The rules from Dutch collective agreements (the cao) are settings you can see and change, every action gets visible feedback, and everything that happens goes into a log you can export at any time.

A site selling that can't hide anything itself. So the price is on the page, the rules are on the page, and nothing on it is invented.

The site is the product

Instead of centred marketing bands, every page uses the app's own shell: a left rail with the navigation, a top bar naming the page and its main action, and a working canvas. The home page states the pitch, shows the price, then puts a real week roster on screen. Visitors see the product's interface before they ever ask for a demo.

Colour means status

The palette came from a screenshot of the product, and with it a rule: colour marks the state of a shift, never decoration. Rust means a person needs to act, amber means it's waiting on someone, teal means it's confirmed. The same three colours mark open shifts on the roster, the buttons that need pressing, and the yes column in the pricing comparison.

Inventing social proof on a site whose whole pitch is honesty would be self-defeating.

From the project's README

Nothing hidden, nothing invented

  • The price calculator is the site's one piece of interaction: the higher of employees × €2 and €20, and it says so when the minimum applies.
  • No testimonials, customer logos or statistics until there are real ones.
  • No analytics, no tracking and no cookies, so there is no cookie banner either.
  • The contact form has no backend: it checks the fields, then hands a filled-in email to your own mail program, and says so next to the button.
  • Placeholders are listed in the README rather than guessed: company details, the privacy page, the share image.

Checked, not assumed

Every text and background pair clears WCAG AA, the status colours included; the tightest is secondary text on a card at 4.9:1. White fails on the rust, which is why the buttons carry a near-black label. The layout holds from 320 pixels wide, and the site reads and navigates without JavaScript.

The product behind it

Shiftbay also exists as a working prototype: a React app that keeps everything in the browser, with a roster, staff, a time clock for a shared tablet, reports and a view for the staff themselves. Before a week is published, it checks every person's shifts for double bookings, too little rest and overtime. It checks against their whole shift history, not just the week on screen, so a Sunday close into a Monday open still gets caught. The clock screen clears itself after thirty seconds, so the next person at the tablet doesn't see someone else's digits or name.

A third shorter

The first version was too long. Every page lost about a third of its copy and vertical spacing, and the audience grew from hospitality alone to every sector that works in shifts.
